# GBP Launch Workbook — Opening & Verifying New Profiles

Companion to `gbp-playbook.md` (which covers optimizing the profiles we already
run). This one covers standing up new ones. Written 2026-08-24, verified against
Google policy as of Aug 2026.

Hosted, printable version: the "GBP Launch Workbook" artifact.

---

## 0. The rule everything hangs on

**One profile per real, staffed location — not one per town served.**

Google grants a profile to a place the business actually operates: own lease or
deed, own staff working posted hours, own signage, own phone line. Not per city,
per technician, or per keyword.

A second profile covering the same territory, same brand, same category reads as
a duplicate. Enforcement lands on the **account**, not just the new profile —
meaning the profile already earning our best leads is the one at risk.

## 1. Gate check — all five must pass before spending a dollar

1. **Do we control the space?** Lease/deed/sublease in the business name. A room
   in someone's house, a relative's garage, a mailbox service, a virtual office,
   or a PO box all fail (PO boxes and virtual offices are explicitly ineligible).
2. **Is it staffed during posted hours?** Someone from the business physically
   there. "The tech is out on jobs all day" means we cannot post staffed hours.
3. **Permanent exterior signage?** Business name, permanently mounted, visible
   from the street, matching the profile name character for character. Not a
   banner for the afternoon, not a truck magnet.
4. **Own phone line and own staff?** Distinct local number ringing at/for that
   location, on its invoices. Two profiles sharing a number is one of the
   fastest duplicate signals Google has.
5. **Genuinely distinct service area?** Substantial overlap with an existing
   profile is duplicating, not expanding. Same county + same category + same
   brand is the textbook duplicate case. Keep areas within ~2h drive.

**Any gate fails, do not open a profile there.** Add the towns to an existing
profile's service-area list (up to 20, free, no risk) instead.

## 2. The only three legitimate ways to add profiles

- **A genuine second branch** — leased, staffed, signed, in a market whose
  service area does not collide with ours.
- **An acquisition** — transfers address history, review corpus, and profile
  age, none of which can be manufactured. Often cheaper per lead than building.
- **A genuinely distinct legal entity** doing distinct work with its own
  location, staff, and licensing. A shell at the same address selling the same
  services is a duplicate with extra paperwork.

Google's multi-profile enforcement is automated, retroactive, and pattern-based:
shared phone numbers, shared landing pages, shared payment instruments, review
author overlap, address clustering. When it fires it hits every connected
profile, including clean ones.

## 3. Higher yield than another profile (ranked)

Profiles produce good leads because of proximity + review strength + category
fit — not pin count. These move those inputs harder, cheaper, at no risk:

1. **Local Services Ads / Google Guaranteed** — sits *above* the map pack, pay
   per lead. We already hold the NY/NJ/CT licensing and insurance it requires.
   Biggest untapped lever we have; scales per market without a new address.
2. **Review velocity with town names in the text** — 100% of completed jobs,
   same day, from the tech's phone. Never incentivize, never gate.
3. **Service areas maxed on existing profiles** — 20 per profile, free, instant.
4. **Categories, services, and products fully populated** — primary category is
   a heavy ranking factor; most competitors leave these half empty.
5. **Weekly real job photos** — original, descriptively named, no stock.
6. **A genuine branch in a non-overlapping market** — cost, not risk. Only once
   1–5 are maxed.
7. **Profiles at addresses that fail the gate check** — account-level risk.
   Short-lived visibility, then removal, taking the clean profiles along.

## 4. Launch checklist for a real location

**Phase 1 — before creating anything** (applying before the paper trail exists
is the top cause of failed verification):

- [ ] Signed lease/deed in the exact legal business name
- [ ] DBA / entity registration filed at that address with the state
- [ ] Dedicated local phone number, live and answered, never shared
- [ ] Utility account or insurance certificate in business name at that address
- [ ] Bank record or invoice template showing the address
- [ ] Permanent exterior signage mounted and photographed
- [ ] Website page for the location live, with that location's phone
- [ ] 3–5 citations built first (Yelp, Facebook, Bing Places, Apple Business
      Connect, Angi) — identical NAP on every one

**Phase 2 — create:**

- [ ] New separate Google account per location; grant main account manager access
- [ ] Business name exactly as on signage — no city, no keywords, no suffixes
- [ ] Primary category set precisely; secondaries after
- [ ] Hours = hours genuinely staffed (do not post 24/7 at an unstaffed unit)
- [ ] Address entered then **hidden** if we dispatch rather than receive customers
- [ ] Service areas set, no meaningful overlap with our other profiles
- [ ] Website + appointment links UTM-tagged (see `gbp-playbook.md` section 1)

**Phase 3 — verify:**

- [ ] Signage clean, address numerals legible from street
- [ ] Branded vehicle on site if we have one
- [ ] Equipment/inventory visible inside — springs, openers, panels, hardware
- [ ] A lockable thing only staff can open, identified *before* recording
- [ ] Daylight, quiet hours, nobody else on site
- [ ] Phone charged, lens wiped, Google Maps app updated

## 5. Buy list (ballpark 2026 US pricing, single small location)

**Signage — non-negotiable**

| Item | Spec that matters | Est. |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior panel sign | Dibond or 1/4 in acrylic, 24x36 in min, name + logo, UV-rated, permanently mounted | $150–350 |
| Address numerals | 4–6 in, high contrast, readable from street | $20–45 |
| Door/window vinyl | Cut vinyl, name + hours, on entry glass | $45–110 |
| Hours placard | Must match profile hours exactly | $20–35 |
| Interior sign/banner | Behind counter or work area | $50–120 |

**Proof of operation**

| Item | Spec that matters | Est. |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle branding | Magnets $60–140/pair; door decals $250–500; partial wrap $1,200–2,500. Name must match exactly | $60–2,500 |
| Branded uniforms | Embroidered shirts x4–6 plus hats | $140–280 |
| Shelving + visible inventory | Real stock: springs, rollers, cables, opener boxes, bins | $180–450 |
| Lockable cabinet | The thing unlocked on camera for proof-of-management | $120–300 |
| Printed collateral | Invoices, letterhead, cards with that address and phone | $60–130 |

**Recurring, per location per month:** space $400–2,000, phone line $25–50,
utility $60–200, insurance rider $30–120.

**Honest math:** ~$850–4,300 up front, $515–2,370/mo. Before committing that,
put the same money into LSA + review velocity on existing profiles for 90 days
and measure. In most home-service markets that produces more booked jobs per
dollar, and it can be switched off.

## 6. Video verification — the single take

Default method for most businesses. One continuous, unedited, **live** recording
made in the Google Maps app on the phone signed into the account that manages
the profile. Cannot be shot separately and uploaded. Min 30s; aim 2–3 min.

Must establish three things: **where the business is**, **that it exists and
operates**, and **that you are authorized to manage it**. Missing proof of
authority is the most common rejection.

Shot sequence — do not stop recording:

| Time | Shot |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Street sign, cross street, recognizable neighbor or landmark |
| 0:15 | Walk toward building; hold on address numerals until legible; tilt to sign |
| 0:30 | Full sign framed with building behind it; branded vehicle if present |
| 0:45 | Walk continuously from exterior through the door — a cut here reads as two locations |
| 1:00 | Interior: workbench, racked inventory, springs, opener boxes, panels, tools |
| 1:45 | **Proof of management** — unlock the cabinet with your key, open the shop computer already signed into our system, pull branded invoices. Show lease/utility bill in business name, **thumb over every account, tax, and ID number** |
| 2:15 | Walk back out, end on sign + address together, stop, submit immediately |

**Do:** daylight, quiet hours, move slowly, hold 3–4s on anything that must be
read, one continuous take, name on sign matches profile character for character.

**Do not:** other people's faces, audible conversation, narration (voices are
disallowed), bank/tax/ID numbers (instant rejection), pausing or restarting
mid-walk, a different device or account than the one managing the profile.

Decision typically within ~5 business days. Rejection is not final — re-record
fixing the specific gap (usually authority evidence or unreadable signage).

## 7. First 90 days after the pin goes live

New profiles carry no trust and no review history and rank poorly until both
accumulate.

- **Week 1** — populate everything: all services, products with UTM links, 20
  service areas, primary + secondary categories, 20+ original photos, full
  description. An empty profile stays invisible.
- **Week 1** — seed Q&A (post from our account, answer from our account —
  permitted, and almost nobody in this trade does it).
- **Weeks 1–4** — reviews above all else; target 10 in month one.
- **Weekly** — 2–3 real job photos + one post; reply to every review within 48h.
- **Week 4** — second citation tier: Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, Nextdoor,
  BBB, manufacturer dealer locators.
- **Week 6** — apply for LSA in that market once reviews hit double digits.
- **Week 12** — pull that profile's calls / direction requests / website clicks
  and compare cost per booked job against existing profiles. That number decides
  whether a third profile is worth it.

## 8. Suspension — triggers and recovery

**Triggers:** keywords or city names in the business name; multiple profiles per
location or territory; an address we do not operate from; two profiles sharing a
phone or landing page; posted hours the location is not staffed for; review
manipulation; editing name/address/category across several profiles at once.

**Prevention:** one profile per location; legal name only; separate Google
account per location; distinct phone and landing page per profile; change one
field at a time with a week between edits; keep lease, utility bill, and dated
signage photos filed.

**If suspended:**

1. Change nothing on the profile — editing during an appeal restarts it.
2. Identify which guideline was actually breached before appealing.
3. Assemble evidence: lease, utility bill in business name, insurance cert,
   dated signage photos, an invoice from that address.
4. File the reinstatement request once with everything attached; wait 3–14 days.
5. If denied, fix the underlying breach first, then re-appeal. Repeated identical
   appeals go nowhere.

Separate accounts per location exist for exactly this: suspensions propagate
along account relationships. Manager access to our main account gives day-to-day
control plus a firebreak.

---

**Scope note.** This covers profiles at locations we genuinely operate. It does
not cover verifying an address that is not a real staffed business location —
that path ends in account-wide removal, and the first profile lost is the one
producing the best leads.
